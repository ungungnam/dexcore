Stage 1 learned a structure-oriented latent z (64-D) of the single-frame canonical contact map; Stage 2 asked whether predicting the future *through* z gives better contact trajectories than predicting the dense map directly, and found that it does not: the dense error of the z-mediated model B1 was <<s2_ec_B1_taco:.3f>> against <<s2_ec_B0_taco:.3f>> for the direct model on TACO (a tie) and <<s2_ec_B1_arctic:.3f>> against <<s2_ec_B0_arctic:.3f>> on ARCTIC (worse). The same report also showed that z is not the weak link as a *representation*: decoding the teacher latents of the GT frames gives a dense error of <<s2_oracle_taco:.2f>> / <<s2_oracle_arctic:.2f>>, about a third of the error of any generated trajectory. What failed was the *prediction* of the latent trajectory.

This study asks why, with two small experiments on the existing representation and nothing else: no new generator, no retraining of B0 / B1 / B2 or of Stage 1, no r, no diffusion, no search. The Stage-1 A3 encoder is frozen; the only things trained are six small regression probes per dataset.
