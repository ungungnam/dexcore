**What is tested.** If the current GT latent z_t is known, how well can z_{t+h} be predicted for h = 1, 4, 8? For every horizon one probe `P_h(z_t, G, τ_t, τ_{t+h}) → ẑ_{t+h}` is trained on all (t, t + h) pairs of the training sequences and scored on all pairs of the test sequences. The probe sees the current latent, the object geometry and the object-trajectory windows of the two frames (which together cover the object states from t − 8 to t + h + 8), and nothing about the future contact, the future latent, the hand, R2 or the wrench (sanity check 4 reproduces all saved test predictions from exactly these four inputs).

{{table1}}

The probe is deliberately strong (9 M parameters for a 64-D regression) and starts as the persistence predictor (zero-initialised residual), so it is judged by what it *adds* to persistence. Capacity is not the limit: every probe reaches its validation optimum after 400–1500 steps (Table 8) and overfits afterwards, i.e. what limits it is generalisation across takes with 1312 / 1005 training sequences. For that reason three closed-form references are reported next to it — per-dimension shrinkage (plain mean reversion), a linear map of z_t alone (a full 64 × 64 ridge: the linear autonomous dynamics of the latent) and a ridge regression on the probe's inputs — together with an ablation probe that does not see the trajectory.
