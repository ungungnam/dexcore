The two datasets agree on the qualitative picture — a smooth latent, a strong ΔC–Δz alignment, an almost empty critical quadrant, a clear gain over persistence for pairs that start at the first frame of a window, and a rescue from the initial latent that fades by frame 8. They differ in two rule inputs: the window-averaged gain of the probe over persistence at h = 4 and 8 (<<p_taco_4_probe_gain_rmse:.1%>> / <<p_taco_8_probe_gain_rmse:.1%>> against <<p_arctic_4_probe_gain_rmse:.1%>> / <<p_arctic_8_probe_gain_rmse:.1%>>) and the rescue from z₀ (<<rescue_taco:.1%>> against <<rescue_arctic:.1%>>, with largely overlapping intervals). The gain alone puts ARCTIC in Case B; the rescue does not enter that branch of the rule. TACO's Case A needs both the clear gain at h = 4 and 8 and a rescue of at least 10 %, and the h = 4 gain and the rescue both sit on their thresholds. On TACO the latent changes faster (persistence R² of z at h = 8 <<p_taco_8_persistence_r2:.2f>> against <<p_arctic_8_persistence_r2:.2f>>; the dense map's is <<g_taco_8_persistence_r2_C:.2f>> against <<g_arctic_8_persistence_r2_C:.2f>>), so there is more for a predictor to gain after the first frames of a window. On ARCTIC persistence is hard to beat from frame 8 on: on test takes of seen and unseen subjects, and one frame ahead on the validation takes too. For pairs starting at t = 1..7 the probe's gain carries over to the seen-subject test takes and not to the subject that is absent from training; at t = 0 it is positive on both, smaller on the unseen subject (<<x_arctic_1_probe_test_unseen_t0_gain_rmse:.0%>> / <<x_arctic_4_probe_test_unseen_t0_gain_rmse:.0%>> / <<x_arctic_8_probe_test_unseen_t0_gain_rmse:.0%>> against <<x_arctic_1_probe_test_seen_t0_gain_rmse:.0%>> / <<x_arctic_4_probe_test_seen_t0_gain_rmse:.0%>> / <<x_arctic_8_probe_test_seen_t0_gain_rmse:.0%>>; Section 7, Table 13).
