* **One seed, one probe family, early-stopped.** Each probe was trained once, reaches its validation optimum after 400–1500 steps (Table 8) and overfits afterwards. The reported gains are what generalises across takes with this recipe and 1312 / 1005 training sequences; they are lower bounds on what is learnable, not estimates of irreducible uncertainty. Three closed-form references (shrinkage, linear map of z_t, ridge on all inputs) are reported as optimisation-free cross-checks; on ARCTIC at h = 1 and 4 the ridge is better than the probe (<<p_arctic_1_linear_gain_rmse:.1%>> [<<p_arctic_1_linear_gain_rmse_lo:.1%>>, <<p_arctic_1_linear_gain_rmse_hi:.1%>>] / <<p_arctic_4_linear_gain_rmse:.1%>> [<<p_arctic_4_linear_gain_rmse_lo:.1%>>, <<p_arctic_4_linear_gain_rmse_hi:.1%>>]); with the ridge the h = 4 label would be *marginal* instead of *no*, and the case (B) would be unchanged. The best predictor at h = 1 is the probe without trajectory (<<best_gain_arctic_1:.1%>>, interval including zero). On TACO the opposite dependence holds: only the probe with the trajectory is *clear* at h = 4; the probe without it (<<p_taco_4_probe_notau_gain_rmse:.1%>> [<<p_taco_4_probe_notau_gain_rmse_lo:.1%>>, <<p_taco_4_probe_notau_gain_rmse_hi:.1%>>]) and the ridge (<<p_taco_4_linear_gain_rmse:.1%>> [<<p_taco_4_linear_gain_rmse_lo:.1%>>, <<p_taco_4_linear_gain_rmse_hi:.1%>>]) are *marginal*, so with either of them in place of the probe the rule would not return Case A.
* **Window averages mix start positions that behave differently.** The pre-registered verdicts use all pairs of a window; the split by window position (Table 12) was added afterwards and shows clear gains at contact onset on both datasets. The ARCTIC Case B and the TACO *clear* label at h = 4 are properties of the averages.
* **Validation interval changed once.** The first launch validated every 500 steps and put the optimum at its first check; all probes were retrained with validation every 100 steps (`logs/first_launch_eval500/`). The decision thresholds were not touched.
* **ARCTIC validation and test takes differ.** The probes' validation gains on ARCTIC do not fully transfer to the test takes (gone at h = 1 and 4, about half left at h = 8). <<x_arctic_4_probe_test_unseen_all_n_takes:.0f>> of the <<n_arctic_test_takes>> test takes come from a subject absent from training; the post-hoc cross split (Table 13) shows that this explains the loss in the first eight frames only. The ARCTIC intervals are wide (<<n_arctic_test_takes>> test takes, <<x_arctic_4_probe_test_unseen_all_n_takes:.0f>> and <<x_arctic_4_probe_test_seen_all_n_takes:.0f>> per subject group), so the cross split is descriptive.
* **Post-hoc analyses.** The split by window position, the subject split and their crossing (Tables 12, 13), the shrinkage and linear references, the paired trajectory contrast (Table 14) and the composition of the exceptional quadrant were added after the test results had been seen; Tables 13 and 14 after two rounds of review of this report. They are interpretation, not verdicts. The rule's inputs and thresholds were not changed. The implementation of the rule (`decide`) was corrected once after review to match the written rule (per-horizon labels, the interval condition); the cases did not change.
* **Trajectory input.** The probe sees the object-state windows of frames t and t + h (object states from t − 8 to t + h + 8 at stride 2), not every intermediate frame at stride 1, and not the whole trajectory that Stage 2 saw.
* **The from-z₀ comparison uses the pooled probe.** Pairs starting at t = 0 are a small share of its training pairs; a predictor fitted for the from-start task alone might do better.
* **No horizon beyond 8 and no closed-loop rollout** (by instruction: no new generator). Statements about what an iterative model could gain at long horizons are hypotheses.
* **Distances.** ΔC is the L2 distance of raw maps (dominated by strongly contacting points), Δz the Euclidean distance of the standardised latent; both are normalised by random-pair distances of training frames. Other metrics would change the numbers of Section 6, hardly its rank statistics. The correlation intervals of Table 4 use 300 bootstrap replicates, all other intervals 1000.
* **Overlapping windows** of the same take share frames; all intervals are take-cluster bootstrap intervals, which accounts for that dependence.
