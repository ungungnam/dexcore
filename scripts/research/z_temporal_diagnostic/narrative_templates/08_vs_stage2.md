**Reading — what was measured and what was not.** "Does access to the current GT latent state dramatically rescue prediction?" Yes when the state is recent: the latent is smooth, and from the middle of a window an eight-frame-old true state gives a <<s_taco_8_all_rescue:.0%>> / <<s_arctic_8_all_rescue:.0%>> lower error than Stage 2. Over the last 15 frames of the window Stage 2 is at <<s2_end_taco:.2f>> / <<s2_end_arctic:.2f>>, against <<s2_end_hold_taco:.2f>> / <<s2_end_hold_arctic:.2f>> for holding z₀ and <<s2_end_mean_taco:.2f>> / <<s2_end_mean_arctic:.2f>> for the train mean over the same frames; over all 63 frames it explains about half (TACO, R² <<s_taco_0_ctx_r2_stage2:.2f>>) or a third (ARCTIC, <<s_arctic_0_ctx_r2_stage2:.2f>>) of the latent's variance.

This is consistent with the view that the difficulty of Stage 2 lies in carrying the latent over many frames without access to its current value, and that the information in (initial state, G, τ) about the latent fades within a few frames. It does not establish it. Nothing was measured from z₀ beyond frame 8; no closed-loop rollout was run, so it is unknown how fast an iterative model's own errors would accumulate; the from-z₀ probe is the pooled probe, for which pairs starting at t = 0 are a small fraction of the training pairs; and both the probes and Stage-2 B1 are early-stopped models trained on 1312 / 1005 sequences, so their errors are upper bounds on the irreducible error (their accuracy is a lower bound on what is predictable), not estimates of it.
